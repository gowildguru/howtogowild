Southwest Has a Better Basic Fare

Airline Comparisons · Published September 2, 2026

The cheapest number in the search results doesn’t always tell you which flight offers the best value. Once you account for a carry-on, cancellation flexibility, and the benefits your status actually unlocks, the comparison can look very different.

That’s why I think Southwest has a better Basic fare—and why Frontier should pay attention when the price difference is small.

Southwest still rewards loyalty on Basic

Southwest Rapid Rewards A-List Preferred members can select available Preferred or Extra Legroom seats at booking, including on Basic fares. Their benefits also include priority check-in and security lanes where available, priority phone support, same-day standby, and up to two premium drinks on eligible flights. Free inflight internet is available where supported. Southwest’s tier and seating benefits.

Even without elite status, Southwest’s Basic fare includes a carry-on and personal item. Complimentary snacks and nonalcoholic drinks are offered on eligible flights. Those little inclusions can make a difference when comparing an everyday travel experience.

The cancellation benefit needs a distinction: Basic is nonrefundable and generally cannot be changed without upgrading the fare. Cancel at least ten minutes before scheduled departure and you may receive an eligible flight credit. Basic credits normally expire six months from the original booking date, not the cancellation date; if the credit is already expired, cancellation may leave no usable value. This is cancellation without a fee—not a promise of a cash refund. Southwest Basic cancellation terms.

Frontier’s changes raise a different question

Frontier has announced changes to elite benefits for bookings made on or after January 1, 2027. Under those changes, premium-seat and UpFront Plus or First Class upgrade benefits depend on buying an eligible bundle. A loyal traveler choosing Basic won’t unlock those seating benefits just by holding status. Frontier’s announced changes and effective dates.

That does not mean Platinum and Diamond members receive no benefits on Basic. Carry-on, boarding, and applicable checked-bag benefits remain valuable. The narrower concern is that Frontier is making some of its most attractive seating perks depend on paying for a bundle, while Southwest lets A-List Preferred members select available Extra Legroom seats even on Basic.

These are upcoming Frontier changes as of this article’s September 2 publication date. They should not be treated as already applying to the October and November 2026 examples below.

What the prices looked like

I compared the same route and travel date, with similar departure times. These are the prices I observed, not guaranteed fares or live quotes.

If Frontier status or a card already covers your bag, your comparison may look different: you might compare against the $33 or $94 Basic price instead. Economy also includes benefits beyond a carry-on, so compare the full bundle with what you actually need.

How small can the gap get before Frontier loses the advantage?

For a traveler who needs to buy a carry-on, $13 or $14 is a modest difference. Southwest’s included onboard refreshments and eligible cancellation credit may be enough to justify it. For an A-List Preferred member, the seating and other tier benefits add another reason to look closely.

Reliability matters, too. In the comparison cited in my original post, Frontier was 1.8 times as likely as Southwest to arrive more than an hour late. A slightly cheaper flight can become expensive if a delay disrupts your plans, so that comparison is another reason to look beyond the price alone.

Frontier can still win on price. But if getting the experience you want requires a paid bundle—and the competing airline is only a few dollars more—how will Frontier keep that traveler’s business?
