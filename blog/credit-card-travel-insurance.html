Which Credit Card Should You Use to Book Your Frontier Flight?

Credit Cards · Published October 5, 2026

When you're booking a $15 GoWild! flight, it's easy to put the charge on whatever card is handy. But the card you use can matter a lot more than the points you'll earn from a $15 purchase.

If that flight is delayed overnight, your bag doesn't arrive, or you have to cancel a trip, the travel protections included with your credit card can potentially save you hundreds—or thousands—of dollars.

I compared the travel insurance benefits of four cards I either carry or think are particularly relevant to Frontier travelers:

  • Capital One Venture X — $395 annual fee
  • Chase Sapphire Reserve — $795 annual fee
  • American Express Platinum — $895 annual fee
  • Frontier Airlines World Mastercard — $99 annual fee

There isn't one card that wins every category, and the annual fee isn't necessarily a good indication of which card has the best coverage.

Trip Delay

For me, this is one of the most important benefits. If a covered delay leaves you stuck somewhere for the night, trip delay insurance can reimburse expenses like a hotel, meals, ground transportation, and toiletries.

The Venture X and Sapphire Reserve both provide up to $500 for a qualifying delay of six hours or more or an overnight delay. Importantly for GoWild! travelers, only a portion of the airfare needs to be purchased with the card to qualify for coverage.

The Amex Platinum also provides up to $500 after a qualifying six-hour delay, but its payment requirements are more restrictive. The full round-trip airfare must be purchased with the card, and there's a limit of two claims per 12-month period.

The Frontier Mastercard doesn't include trip delay coverage.

Baggage Delay

This was probably the biggest surprise in my comparison.

The Sapphire Reserve covers up to $100 per day for five days when checked baggage is delayed.

Neither the Venture X nor Amex Platinum provides baggage delay coverage.

The $99 Frontier Mastercard does.

It provides up to $100 per day after a four-hour baggage delay, with additional coverage based on the length of the delay. That's a useful benefit on a relatively inexpensive airline card—and one that two significantly more expensive premium cards don't provide.

Lost or Damaged Baggage

Baggage delay and lost baggage are two different benefits. If your suitcase eventually arrives tomorrow, that's a baggage delay. If the airline actually loses or damages it, you're looking at lost baggage coverage.

The Venture X and Sapphire Reserve both provide up to $3,000 per covered trip. The Amex Platinum provides up to $2,000, while the Frontier Mastercard doesn't provide this coverage.

There are additional limits for things like jewelry and electronics, so the headline limit isn't necessarily what you'd receive for every claim.

Trip Cancellation and Interruption

This is where the differences get much larger.

These benefits can reimburse eligible prepaid, non-refundable expenses when you have to cancel or cut a trip short for a reason specifically covered by the policy.

The Venture X provides up to $2,000 per covered trip.

The Frontier Mastercard provides up to $1,500 per covered trip, which is fairly respectable for a $99 card.

Amex provides up to $10,000 per trip, with a combined cancellation and interruption maximum of $20,000 per 12-month period.

The Sapphire Reserve has the strongest coverage of the four: up to $10,000 per traveler and $20,000 per trip, subject to a combined $40,000 annual maximum.

That difference may not matter much if you're taking a cheap weekend trip. It could matter enormously if your $15 Frontier flight is just one piece of a trip with several thousand dollars in prepaid hotels, cruises, tours, or other expenses.

Rental Car Coverage

Venture X and Sapphire Reserve both provide primary rental-car coverage with coverage up to $75,000.

The Amex Platinum includes secondary coverage, meaning other applicable insurance generally comes into play first. The Frontier Mastercard doesn't include rental-car coverage.

For me, primary coverage is a major advantage. I don't want to involve my personal auto insurance in a rental-car claim if I don't have to.

So Which Card Am I Using?

There's no universal answer.

The Chase Sapphire Reserve has the strongest overall travel protections of these four cards. It covers essentially every category I compared and has particularly strong cancellation, interruption, and baggage-delay coverage.

The Venture X is a strong middle ground. At a substantially lower annual fee, it still gives me the protections I care about most for many trips: trip delay, lost baggage, and primary rental-car coverage. Its biggest omission is baggage delay.

The Frontier Mastercard surprised me. It isn't a replacement for a premium travel card, but baggage delay and $1,500 of cancellation/interruption coverage are meaningful benefits on a $99 card.

And the Amex Platinum is a good reminder not to judge travel insurance by a card's annual fee. It's an expensive card with plenty of other benefits, but strictly from a travel insurance perspective, there are several situations where I'd rather have paid with Venture X or Sapphire Reserve.

For a typical GoWild! flight, I'm probably reaching for the Venture X or Sapphire Reserve.

When the flight costs $15, earning an extra point per dollar doesn't matter very much. The difference between earning 30 points and 45 points certainly isn't going to change my life.

Having someone pick up a $400 hotel bill when my flight gets delayed overnight might.

Credit card insurance benefits have eligibility requirements, exclusions, and coverage limits. Benefits can change. This comparison is intended as a general overview and not a guarantee of coverage. Always review your card's current Guide to Benefits before relying on a particular protection.
