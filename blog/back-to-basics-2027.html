Back To “Basics”: How Airlines and Their Evolving Loyalty Programs Are Competing for Your Dollars in 2027.

Airline Comparisons · Published October 15, 2026

Southwest Airlines announced sweeping changes to its Rapid Rewards loyalty program today (October 15, 2026), just days after United Airlines announced more rigorous qualifiers for its MileagePlus Premier Status. And less than a month ago, Frontier Airlines announced costly changes to its loyalty program—requiring members to purchase bundles at an added cost to receive benefits they were promised previously. The question remains: which airline comes out on top when it comes to finding value for your air travel needs?

I can’t say that I’m surprised by the changes Southwest announced. Southwest lost its value edge last year when private equity took over. The Elliott group forced major changes that took away benefits loyal customers expected and “luved.” Two free checked bags were replaced with credit card membership requirements, and open seating was replaced with assigned seating. With this push to be like the “Big Guys” came the push toward a more premium experience. We’ve already seen the introduction of Extra Legroom seats, free Wi-Fi, in-seat power, elevated snacks, interline agreements with carriers around the world, and new fare types. Southwest has also announced upcoming enhancements to its brand and customer experience with new status tiers, Southwest “Icon” Lounges, and a premium credit card. While Southwest left its identity as a low-cost carrier behind in the chase for profits, it didn’t erase its entire customer experience. Southwest still rewards its customers with perks like a free carry-on, no cancellation fees, an inflight snack and nonalcoholic beverage, and Rapid Rewards perks on all fare types.

Conversely, after the quite-expected death of Spirit Airlines, Frontier was faced with an identity crisis: remain the country’s ultra-low-cost leader or give in to the greed of other airlines’ practices and demand more money from its already loyal customer base. Well, we all know how that ended… From my own observations, beyond the announced loyalty program changes, I’ve seen sneaky, unannounced changes that are squeezing loyal customers even more.

  • Row 3 on all flights is now sold as UpFront Plus, no longer a premium seat bookable with current elite perks, removing six premium seats from the map.
  • Discount Den is worthless. In a sample of over a dozen flights on multiple dates and multiple routes, Discount Den saves a whopping one dollar.
  • The cash prices for Economy bundles on GoWild! fares are consistently higher than the bundle prices for any other fare type.

This is on top of announced changes. So not only will you be forced to buy a bundled fare type to get any onboard perks like seat assignment, but you will also be charged more than the person sitting next to you on another fare type because the airline knows you’ll pay it.

It almost feels as if some executive at Frontier said, “Corner them into paying us more, and since they’re loyal, let’s charge them more!” (as they sneered and rubbed their greedy hands together).

Does Frontier still present great value at times? Yes! It always will. But in such a competitive landscape, at a certain point you have to ask when the abysmal travel experience it offers will outweigh the cost savings. With new “premium-looking” branding, a new app (that is still riddled with errors, misinformation, and constantly reported glitches), and a new loyalty program, Frontier is coming in hot with a “Premium Experience”—but fails to deliver when you actually show up at the airport or call customer support.

Frontier can still be a great deal. But if I’m paying more to use the perks I already earned, I’m going to look at what the competition offers. A new logo and a “premium” label aren’t going to cut it. If airlines want our loyalty, they need to give us a reason to stay.
