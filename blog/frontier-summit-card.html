    What Would Make Me Pay $249 for a Premium Frontier Card?

    Frontier Credit Cards · Opinion · Published November 20, 2026

    I’m writing this from a Priority Pass lounge. As much as I enjoy a free snack before my flight, trip delay reimbursement is probably the biggest reason I renew my Capital One Venture X. It’s also why I book so many flights with it, including flights on Frontier.

    I’ve already used that coverage after Frontier stranded me somewhere on a GoWild! ticket. I know. Try to contain your surprise.

    I fly Frontier a lot, and I already have its credit card. When it’s time to book a flight, though, I usually reach for something else. If Frontier wants me to pay $249 a year for a premium card, it has to compete with the benefits I’m already using.

    Frontier is already making a push into premium

    Frontier is installing First Class seats, selling them for travel starting March 5, 2027, and revamping Frontier Miles. Southwest has announced its first airport lounges and a new premium Chase card that will get cardholders into them. Frontier’s cabin plans · Frontier Miles changes · Southwest’s announcement

    Frontier Airlines is currently the largest airline in The United States without a Premium Cobrand Credit Card. BTS domestic market share by revenue passenger miles

    The current $99 Frontier Airlines World Mastercard can be a decent deal. It offers two free checked bags for the primary cardholder on eligible Frontier flights, priority boarding, and a $100 anniversary flight voucher after $2,500 in purchases during the cardmember year. Cardholders also earn more miles and status points on Frontier bookings under the new rules, which helps when the basic-fare earn rates are this punishing. For the way I travel, it still comes up short against the cards I already use. Frontier’s card benefits

    The first thing I’d look for is trip delay reimbursement

    If Frontier strands me somewhere overnight, I want the card I booked with to help cover a hotel and a meal. I’d want up to $500 per eligible ticket after a covered delay of more than six hours or one that requires an overnight stay, along with terms that make it clear that GoWild! bookings can qualify when I pay for them with the card.

    That’s the standard I’d compare it to because it’s already a big part of why my Venture X gets so much use. I’d check the trip delay terms before I even looked at the welcome bonus. Capital One’s Visa Infinite benefits guide

    What else would make the Summit Card worth $249?

    I’d call it the Frontier Summit Card, a nod to Summit, the top tier of Frontier’s old EarlyReturns program. The name, the mockup, and the benefits here are my own concept. Frontier’s annual report on EarlyReturns

    • Discount Den included. For $249 a year, I’d want access to Frontier’s member fares included.
    • Automatic Silver Elite status. I’d also take a higher rate for earning status points on card spending, paired with an annual head start toward status.
    • Priority Pass Select. Four included lounge visits a year would be a reasonable starting point. I’d use them.
    • Annual companion or upgrade certificates. Give me two companion certificates I can use on trips I’d already book, or a couple of upgrades I can confirm when I book Frontier First.
    • The perks the current card gets right. Keep the two free checked bags, priority boarding, and the chance to earn the anniversary voucher.

    Would it win me over?

    Frontier already gets my business when the fare makes sense. Discount Den and companion certificates could make a $249 card worth keeping. Trip delay reimbursement that works on my GoWild! bookings is what would get me to pull it out and actually pay for my next Frontier flight.

    What would make you reach for a Frontier card when you book your next Frontier flight?
