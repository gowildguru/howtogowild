GoWild! Availability

Understand when to book, how promotional fares work, and why GoWild! may not appear on every flight.

Standard Booking

For domestic flights, GoWild! fares become eligible for confirmed booking without an Early Booking Fee starting at midnight the day before departure, in the departure airport’s local time zone.

This rule applies to every flight segment on a booking. An itinerary connecting through a different time zone is fully within the standard booking window only when all segments depart today or tomorrow in their respective departure airport time zones.

For example, an Orlando–Denver–Las Vegas itinerary may need to wait until midnight in Denver—2:00 a.m. in Orlando—before every segment is within its standard booking window.

An open booking window does not guarantee availability. Taxes and applicable charges still apply. International flights have a separate standard booking window.

Early Booking

What is Early Booking?

On a promotional basis, GoWild! fares may become available beyond the standard one-day domestic booking window. These promotions have often appeared for off-peak travel dates.

Promotional fares may allow you to book flights weeks, or occasionally months, in advance with $0.01 in airfare. Significant Early Booking Fees may apply, in addition to taxes and other applicable charges. Promotional availability is not guaranteed.

Why is GoWild! Early Booking more expensive than the Discount Den fare?

GoWild! and Discount Den are separate membership programs with different offers and fare availability. If you have both memberships, compare the total prices and book the lowest eligible fare you see.

Does a lack of Early Booking mean GoWild! won’t be available during the standard booking window?

No. Early Booking uses limited promotional inventory. A flight without an Early Booking fare may still offer GoWild! during the standard booking window, but availability is not guaranteed.

Peak Day Booking

Although GoWild! has published blackout dates, some Early Booking promotions may offer GoWild! fares on those dates with a Peak Day charge. Inventory is limited.

The Peak Day charge does not disappear when the standard booking window opens. Do not expect a promotional blackout-date fare to drop to the usual standard-window price.

In the fare breakdown described by passholders, the charge appears under GWEF. Select the fare and expand its taxes and fees to review the charge and total price before booking.

Flight Availability

Load Factor

Historically, passholders have observed that GoWild! fares are less likely to appear when roughly 15 or fewer seats remain. This is a community observation, not a published Frontier cutoff. Other inventory controls can affect availability.

A seat map does not reliably show how many seats are unsold. Some ticketed passengers do not yet have assigned seats, and some seats may be blocked from selection.

Missing GoWild! fares can reflect limited inventory or other restrictions—even when seats are still available to purchase.

Demand

High-demand flights may not offer GoWild! fares. Seats available for purchase do not guarantee GoWild! availability.

Passholders have reported difficulty finding GoWild! fares in markets such as New York City and Southern California. These reports describe traveler experiences rather than a published market restriction.

Availability can vary by route, date, and departure time. Keeping your plans flexible gives you more options to check.
